## Page Boundary Contract

A page's responsibility boundary — what it owns, what it excludes, and who owns each excluded concern — written only as prose is not machine-checkable, and its absence carries no meaning. The `boundary` frontmatter block makes the enumerable half of that boundary a controlled structure. This leaf owns the block's schema, its cross-page rules, and its projection. It does not judge whether a boundary is drawn correctly — that stays with semantic review — and it never carries any instance's concern vocabulary.

- `owns`: nonempty list; each entry is a kebab-case concern slug, or a one-key mapping from a slug to a nonempty list of sub-slugs. Top-level and sub-slugs are equally referenceable concerns.
- `excludes`: optional list; each entry carries exactly `concern` (a slug) and `owner` (a vault page reference, with or without the `.md` suffix).
- `goals` / `non_goals`: optional lists of nonempty strings. They carry no cross-page semantics; only their shape is checked.

Self-consistency: no slug appears both in `owns` (at either level) and as an excluded `concern`; no `owner` names the declaring page itself; no slug repeats inside `owns`.

## Cross-page Rules

- Resolvability: every `excludes[].owner` resolves to an existing page inside the vault.
- Reciprocity: the owner page's own `boundary.owns` contains the excluded concern at either level. An owner page that carries no `boundary` block at all is a migration-tolerated candidate, never a hard failure even in strict mode; an owner that carries the block but does not claim the concern is a violation.
- Uniqueness: one concern slug is owned by at most one page corpus-wide — the content-plane mapping of the single-owner principle. Absence thereby becomes meaningful: an unclaimed concern is deterministically unowned, not possibly-forgotten.

Concern slugs MAY be closed by the selected profile through the existing vocabulary extension mechanism; membership legality stays with the `frontmatter-vocabulary` gate, and this contract never re-checks enums. Without a declared vocabulary, only the slug shape is checked.

## Projection

The block between `<!-- boundary-projection:begin -->` and `<!-- boundary-projection:end -->` is the human-readable projection of the `boundary` block, generated by `Tools/render_boundary_projection.py` and tool-owned under the [[kernel/K08 Metadata and Status/07 Frontmatter Writer and Projection Authority|K08/07]] authority rules: regenerated, never hand-edited. Marker placement is curated — the author decides where the block sits; the tool owns only its content. A page without markers is skipped, never treated as stale. The default display labels are owned here — `Owns`, `Not owned here`, `Owner`, `Goals`, `Non-goals`, and the generated preamble sentence — and the selected profile MAY override any of them through the `boundary_projection.labels` mapping of its `Metadata Contract` slot; an override changes display text only, never schema or semantics.

## Enablement

`Tools/check_boundary_contract.py` is the `boundary-contract` gate and ships advisory under the same [[kernel/K08 Metadata and Status/06 Frontmatter Applicability Contract|K08/06]] Enablement rule: violations are candidates that support migration planning, and promotion to a blocking gate is a separate governance decision under [[kernel/K12 Quality Assurance/10 Standards Version Adoption|K12/10]]. In the applicability base the `boundary` field is `optional` with shape `delegated`: presence, mode, and the unknown-field closure stay with the `page-contract` gate; the block's internal structure is owned here and checked only by `boundary-contract`. A profile MAY tighten the field's mode per page context; it MUST NOT redefine the block's internal schema.
