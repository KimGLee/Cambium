## Structural Unit Interface

A structural unit is a registered knowledge responsibility with a stable identity, not a directory that happens to exist. Two kinds are defined:

- `domain`: a stable, independent knowledge responsibility registered at the top of the selected profile's layer architecture.
- `module`: a sub-responsibility of one parent unit that is built, maintained, or audited independently.

A directory becomes a `module` only when all of the following hold; otherwise it remains an ordinary grouping covered by its parent's entry:

1. It carries a unique responsibility no sibling unit carries.
2. It has one canonical entry page.
3. It has exactly one explicit parent unit.
4. Coverage can project its pages as a set.
5. It is genuinely maintained or audited on its own.
6. It is not a synonym-level split of its parent.

Plain grouping, attachment, archive, or file-count-control directories MUST NOT be registered as modules. Queue order, file counts, or the fact that sibling directories have entry pages MUST NOT decide structure.

## Unit Roles And Implementation Modes

Each registered unit binds one required `entry` role and declares the four optional roles: `sequence`, `coverage` projection, `quick_reference`, and `expression` artifacts. Every declared role states exactly one implementation mode:

- `embedded`: carried by a section of another page; requires a path and a heading.
- `standalone`: an independent page; requires a path.
- `derived`: generated by a deterministic tool; requires the generator and the owner of its inputs, and MAY additionally name the path and heading where the projection renders (a derived writer with an embedded presentation). A derived view is regenerated or invalidated when its inputs change and is never a canonical owner.
- `not-applicable`: requires a stated structural reason.

Absence of a declaration MUST NOT be used to express `not-applicable`. One page MAY legitimately implement several roles (an Overview with an embedded sequence), but the registry states the combination explicitly; a matching heading alone proves nothing. An expression artifact binds to a canonical unit and MUST NOT become that unit's canonical owner.

## Registry Contract

The selected profile registers its units in the `Structure Registry` slot, one restricted-YAML document validated by `Tools/check_structure.py`. Ownership boundaries:

- `Profile Scope` remains the sole owner of Layer IDs, top-level directory sets, and layer responsibilities.
- The Structure Registry owns only per-unit identity, parent, root, entry, and role bindings — for domains, modules, and the support layers of [[kernel/K01 Scope and Architecture/06 Support Layer Structural Interfaces|K01/06]].
- The Global Map registers important entries and explicit typed relations; it does not copy the registry.
- Coverage owns accepted pages; each knowledge object records only its single finest-grained structural owner, and parent domains are derived through the registry's parent graph, never maintained twice.
- The registry MUST NOT hold page completion, batch state, holds, receipts, or statistics snapshots.

The `structure-registry` gate is the registry's sole deterministic producer: it verifies unit-ID uniqueness, an acyclic parent graph, resolvable paths and headings, explicit role modes with their per-mode required declarations, Global Map bindings, and Coverage unit references, and it fails closed on an empty or unreadable registry. It proves structure declarations only, never content quality; module acceptance stays with [[kernel/K12 Quality Assurance/03 Module and Coverage Review|Module and Coverage Review]]. Routes that build modules, migrate structure, or reconcile planning artifacts consume a current receipt for this gate; an existing adopter enters it through Standards adoption under [[kernel/K12 Quality Assurance/10 Standards Version Adoption|K12/10]].
